How to Measure Free Fire Ping Before and After Connecting to a Proxy Server

Accurately measuring latency before and after connecting helps players evaluate whether a connection change makes a meaningful difference. Using ff proxy server apk as part of a controlled test, compare ping under similar network conditions and record the results rather than relying on a single match.

Measuring ping is not a one-time check. A single match tells you what happened in one session under one set of conditions — it does not tell you whether a proxy consistently improves or worsens your connection. A reliable measurement requires a repeatable process: establish a baseline, connect through the proxy, repeat the same tests, and compare across multiple matches before drawing any conclusion.

A Repeatable Testing Method

1

Record Baseline Ping

Before connecting to any proxy, play several Free Fire matches on your usual network. Record the ping displayed in-game during each match, noting the time, network type, and any unusual conditions.

2

Connect to the Proxy

After recording a sufficient baseline, connect through the proxy using the same device and network. Ensure no other settings have changed — same Wi-Fi or mobile data, same location, same time of day if possible.

3

Repeat the Same Tests

Play the same number of matches under conditions as similar as possible to the baseline tests. Record the same information — ping readings, time, network, and any observations about stability.

4

Compare Average and Fluctuations

Calculate the average ping for both sets of tests, but also look at the range — how much ping varied within each match. A lower average with wider fluctuations may feel worse than a slightly higher average that stays steady.

How to Interpret the Results

Average ping is only one metric. A proxy that reduces average ping by 10ms but introduces wider swings may feel less stable during gameplay. Pay equal attention to how much the ping fluctuates — consistency often matters more than the single best reading.
  • Run at least three to five matches for both baseline and proxy tests
  • Keep network type, device, and location consistent between tests
  • Record time of day to account for potential congestion variation
  • Compare both average ping and the range of fluctuation
  • Do not conclude from a single unusually fast or slow match
